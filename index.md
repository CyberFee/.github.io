Welcome to my GitHub page! This is where my work, research, and approach to data come together. I'll start with what I believe.

Data is more than metrics and algorithms. Data tells the stories that otherwise go untold: whose neighborhood gets labeled "high risk," whose symptoms get flagged, whose journey gets counted. I approach data with a deep respect for what it can do, because the same numbers that can open doors for a community can quietly close them. My work is about using data to illuminate and repair, not to reinforce what's already unjust.

### What I do
 
I'm the **Director of Business Intelligence and Analytics at Spelman College**, an institution whose students helped lead the Atlanta Student Movement, named injustice plainly in *An Appeal for Human Rights*, and demanded divestment from apartheid. That history shapes how I see my work. I build the tools and resources that make institutional decisions more transparent and accountable, so that data can do what those students did: name what is true and insist that it be acted on.

### What I'm researching
 
My work sits at the intersection of data science, statistics, and machine learning, with an equity-focused lens. I examine how models used in neighborhood risk classification, clinical pre-screening, and public health research can encode and amplify existing inequities, particularly for Black and marginalized communities. My published work and ongoing projects look at algorithmic systems in healthcare, housing, and public policy.
 
Currently, I am extending this equity lens to immigration trends through the framework of the African diaspora, exploring how thoughtful data modeling can illuminate a more complete, nuanced, and just narrative.

___

### Research & Impact

✦ **Translating Complexity:** Built policy-oriented ML analyses of gentrification and displacement in Atlanta — translating model outputs into neighborhood typologies useful for city planners and advocates.  

⚖ **Equity-First Analysis:** Investigated bias and ethical limitations in AI-based diabetes risk screening tools, and identified data precision gaps in CDC mortality data that systematically underrepresent marginalized populations.

⛁ **Applied Data Analysis & Institutional Research:** Developed models and evaluation frameworks for leadeship decision making at an HBCU — translating complex, multi-source data into clear insights
  
♟ **Public-Interest Research:** Co-authored published research predicting adverse childhood experiences using ML.  Currently examining immigration trends through the lens of the African diaspora.

### How I think about data
 
I'm a student of this craft and plan to stay one. The field moves fast, and the most important questions require learning from statisticians, clinicians, historians, and the communities the data describes. I also see poetry in data. Finding the pattern that is true and presenting it so people can feel it takes as much care as any sentence.

### Featured Academic Research
Check out my repositories below to see how I translate technical findings into social impact narratives.  

**EQUITY FOCUSED DATA STORY**


* **[Under the Amazon Effect: An Equity Lens](datastory.html)**  
Joining demographic census data and numerous outcome variables (socioeconomic, built environment, enviornmental data)to spatial data to explore the impact of corporate development on South Fulton, GA.


**IMMIGRATION POLICY FOCUSED DATA STORY**

* **[Bordered Bodies: Mapping Detention, Diaspora and the Geographies of Black Immmigration in America](datastory_immi.html)**  
Using ICE detention and apprehension data along with Census figures, this project assesses the impact of recent immigration policy changes on African Diaspora populations in the U.S.


**COMPUTATIONAL SOCIAL JUSTICE**

* **[Code of (In)Justice: Algorithmic Bias in Pretrial Risk Assessmen](social_justice.md)**  
Research project examining racial and socioeconomic bias in generative AI-powered bail prediction. Used priming conditions and Georgia bail reform data to show how ChatGPT reproduces discriminatory patterns from historical criminal justice data, despite claims of algorithmic neutrality.



**COMPUTATIONAL SOCIAL JUSTICE & ETHICAL ADVOCACY**

* **[Linguistic Diversity and Social Justice in ML, NLP and LLMs](social_justice_ethics.md)**  
Research project examining racial and socioeconomic bias in generative AI-powered bail prediction. Used priming conditions and Georgia bail reform data to show how ChatGPT reproduces discriminatory patterns from historical criminal justice data, despite claims of algorithmic neutrality.

**HOUSING & POLICY**

* **[Gentrification & Displacement Risk in Atlanta](ml_predicmodel.md)**
Multi-phase, policy-oriented ML analysis of neighborhood change and displacement risk across the Atlanta metro area. Compared classification models, modeled displacement magnitude, and used K-means clustering to validate neighborhood typologies for policy intervention.

**HEALTH EQUITY & AI ETHICS**

* **AI-Based Diagnostic Support for Diabetes Risk**
Co-developed an interpretability-focused pre-screening tool using NHANES data (~3,700 records) to flag elevated diabetes risk. Centered bias awareness and ethical constraints on deploying ML in high-stakes clinical contexts.

**PUBLIC HEALTH & STRUCTURAL INEQUITY**

* **Racial Inequities in Disease Mortality**
As part of a genomics research group, analyzed CDC Wonder mortaility data to identify systemic gaps disproportionaely affecting marginalized populations. Used Bayesian clustering and AI-assisted literature synthesis to surface sociostructural drivers of health disparities for policy and advocacy use.

**IMMIGRATION & AFRICAN DIASPORA**

* **Immigration Trends Through the Lens of the African Diaspora**
Ongoing research examining current U.S. immigration trends through the framework of the African diaspora — bringing a data science lens to questions of displacement, belonging, and policy impact that are often told without quantitative grounding.

**PUBLICATION(S)**

Tawiah, N. A., Appiah, E. A., & White, F. (2025). Predicting adverse childhood experiences from family environment factors: A machine learning approach. Behavioral Sciences, 15(9), 1216.

Peer-reviewed research applying ML classification to predict ACEs from family environment data with implications for early intervention policy and child welfare systems.
